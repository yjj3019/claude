# Writing Policy

## General

Write clearly, directly, and technically.

Prefer:

- the outcome first, especially after long-running work
- precise statements
- structured sections
- reusable wording
- operational relevance
- complete sentences that do not depend on temporary working shorthand

Avoid:

- marketing fluff
- vague value claims
- unsupported superlatives
- excessive abstraction

## Editing Korean prose

- Distinguish confirmed spelling or spacing errors from permitted variants and optional style suggestions; consult authoritative language guidance when a rule is uncertain. If the rule cannot be verified or sources disagree, state the uncertainty rather than asserting an error.
- Preserve the author's meaning, supported facts, uncertainty and intended voice; do not invent facts or strengthen claims during editing. Match the supplied writing sample and formality when appropriate; avoid mechanical removal of expressions that serve the context.
- When editing prose, preserve commands, code, configuration keys, paths, link targets and test inputs unless the requested change explicitly includes them.

## Enterprise Writing

Every enterprise artifact should answer:

- What problem is being solved?
- Why does it matter?
- What is proposed?
- What are the risks?
- How is success validated?
- What should the reader do next?
